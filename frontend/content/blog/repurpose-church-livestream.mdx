Your church already produces more content in ninety minutes on a Sunday morning than most brands produce in a month. The problem is that it exists as one 90-minute file that roughly eleven people will ever watch to the end.

This is the workflow for turning that file into a week. It assumes a volunteer team, not a staff media department, and it assumes nobody involved has four spare hours.

## What's actually in the file

A typical service recording contains more distinct assets than most teams realize:

- The sermon (30–45 min) — your primary source
- The scripture reading — usually a clean, quotable 60–90 seconds
- Any testimony or interview — often the single best-performing content of the whole service
- Announcements — mostly not useful for social, see below
- Worship — handle with care, for licensing reasons covered in [sermon clipping permissions](/blog/sermon-clipping-permissions)

Ninety percent of what you publish will come from the sermon and the testimony. Plan accordingly and stop trying to make announcements work.

## The week, mapped

Here's the shape. Adjust days to fit your team, but keep the *density* — roughly four posts, front-loaded early in the week while the service is still fresh.

**Sunday afternoon — the same-day clip.** One clip, published within a few hours of service ending. This is the highest-value post of the week and the one most churches skip because everyone's tired.

It matters because your congregation is still thinking about it. They were there three hours ago. This is the post that gets shared to personal accounts, which is the only distribution mechanism that reliably reaches outside your existing following.

Keep it simple. It does not need to be your best clip. It needs to exist by 4pm.

**Tuesday — the second sermon clip.** A different moment from the same sermon, ideally a different *type* of moment. If Sunday's was a story, Tuesday's should be practical instruction or a hard question.

**Thursday — the archive clip.** This is the step that changes everything about sustainability, and almost nobody does it.

Pull something from three, six, or twelve months ago. Nobody remembers it. To anyone who joined since, it's brand new. To anyone who was there, it lands differently the second time.

Once you've been clipping for six months, Thursday costs you almost nothing — you're drawing from a library that already exists. This is how a team survives a bad week: the archive covers you.

**Saturday — the invitation.** Short, warm, pointed at tomorrow. A 20-second clip of the pastor saying "we'd love to have you," or a still with service times. Low effort by design.

That's four posts. Two require new work, one is archive, one is a template you rebuild in ten minutes.

## The actual production run

Here's where the hours go and how to compress them.

**Export and hand off (10 min).** Whoever runs the stream drops the file somewhere the media person can get it. This handoff is the number one point of failure in church media workflows — not editing, not equipment. Someone has to own it and it has to happen Sunday, not Wednesday.

Make sure the file has board audio, not room audio. If your archived copy is worse than your stream, fix that once and it stays fixed forever.

**Transcribe (5 min, mostly waiting).** Reading is roughly four times faster than watching. Do not skip this and try to scrub through the video — that's where the four-hour afternoons come from.

**Select (15 min).** Scan the transcript, mark eight to twelve candidates, apply the standalone test: read it out loud with nothing before or after. If it needs context, cut it. The checklist for what to look for is in [20 sermon moments worth clipping](/blog/sermon-clip-ideas).

**Cut and reframe (30–60 min for 4 clips).** Trim to the clip boundaries, convert 16:9 to 9:16. If your pastor stays behind a pulpit, a static center crop is fine. If they move, you need either keyframed crops or automatic speaker tracking, or you'll spend the bulk of your time here.

**Caption and correct (20 min).** Auto-generate, then fix proper nouns. Biblical names get mangled every single time. Keep a running correction list for your church's recurring terms — speaker names, book names, your church's name — and it gets faster every week.

**Write copy (15 min).** Four captions. Hook line that survives truncation, two lines of context, scripture reference, one specific ask.

**Publish (10 min, or 40 min the hard way).** This is the step that's deceptively expensive. Posting four clips to Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, and Threads individually means twenty separate upload flows, each with its own quirks about aspect ratio, caption length, and file size. Done manually it's most of an evening.

Total, done well: **about two hours.** Done without transcription and without scheduled publishing: four to five.

## Two volunteers, not five

The way this actually stays alive long-term is splitting it into two roles that don't need to be in the same room.

**The selector** watches or reads the sermon and picks the moments. This person needs judgment and needs to know the church — ideally someone who's been there a while and understands what's pastorally appropriate to send out. It's about 25 minutes of work.

**The producer** cuts, captions, and publishes. This is the mechanical role and it can be someone with no theological background at all — a teenager who's good at CapCut is genuinely great at this.

Splitting it this way means neither person is doing four hours, and it means losing one volunteer doesn't kill the whole operation. Most church media teams collapse because one person was doing everything and then they had a baby.

## The realistic version

If you're starting from nothing, don't build the four-post week in week one. Build it in this order:

1. **Weeks 1–4:** Sunday clip only. One post. Prove the handoff works and that someone will actually do it every week.
2. **Weeks 5–8:** Add Tuesday. Now you're pulling two clips per sermon.
3. **Month 3:** Add Saturday's invitation post. It's a template, so it costs almost nothing.
4. **Month 6:** Add Thursday's archive clip — by now you have an archive worth pulling from.

Six months to a full week of content, built on a foundation that doesn't break when one person gets sick.

The alternative — launching all four posts in week one — is the approach almost every church takes, and it's why so many church accounts have twelve posts from one enthusiastic March and nothing since.
